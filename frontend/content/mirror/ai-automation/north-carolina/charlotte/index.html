Charlotte at a glance

A banking town that runs on small business.

Charlotte is the second-largest banking center in the United States, behind only New York, and home to the headquarters of Bank of America and Truist, with Wells Fargo running major East Coast operations here. But the day-to-day economy runs on the firms around those towers: the suppliers, trades, logistics companies, and service businesses that keep the city moving. Mecklenburg County employment grew 6.8% in a single recent year, and growth like that strains follow-up, quoting, and scheduling first. That strain is where we come in.

Mecklenburg County

Charlotte's business base

The banks get the headlines. Restaurants, trades and distributors do the hiring.

Mecklenburg County had 36,472 business locations in 2023, and 10,746 of them had between 10 and 499 employees. The biggest group in that size band was restaurants and food service, with 1,740 locations. Retail had 1,415, professional and technical firms 900, wholesalers 761, construction 667 and administrative and support services 652.

Those are the businesses that live and die on follow-up. A missed call, a quote that goes out a day late or an invoice nobody chases costs them more than it costs a bank. That's the work we build for: answer the lead, follow up, quote, invoice, chase, and keep it all running.

AI that runs on hardware you own, inside your building, for work that's too sensitive for public AI tools. Your data never leaves your walls. Built for firms with strict privacy or compliance rules. That matters around here, where plenty of firms handle bank or insurance data under strict rules.
07

A clear view of what your business spends on AI tools and tokens, where it's wasted, and limits that keep it in check. Model-agnostic, so you can switch AI models later without a rebuild.

Rules to know

AI and data rules in North Carolina.

As of September 2026, North Carolina has not passed a broad AI law or consumer-privacy law that applies to private businesses. Federal rules and your industry's rules still apply.

If you handle health, financial, or children's data, keep it out of public AI tools unless your vendor contract covers it. Private, on-site AI is an option when it can't leave the building.

This is a plain-English summary, not legal advice.
