Systems and data in Haywood County

Where Haywood County records come apart.

A Haywood County business usually runs on whatever grew up with it: a point-of-sale system here, a spreadsheet there, a booking tool set up years ago and never revisited. With 1,546 business locations in the county and most of them small, nobody has an IT department keeping the records straight.

So the same guest, customer, or job exists three times, spelled three ways, and the numbers never quite agree. Every report becomes an argument. The foundation work ends that: one record, one place it lives, and systems that hand work to each other instead of to a person with a keyboard.

It holds for the whole county: the records of a Fines Creek farm operation, a Dellwood builder, and a Saunook shop all end up in the same three places if nobody fixes the foundation, and the fix is the same.

What the foundation work covers

You can't put AI on top of a mess.

01

One CRM instead of three

Every customer, guest, and job in one place, with the duplicates merged and the dead records gone. Your team stops asking which list is right.
02

Records that match

Names, addresses, prices, and job histories cleaned and lined up across your tools, so a quote, an invoice, and a report all describe the same work.
03

The Company Brain, built on clean ground

On top of matched records we build the Company Brain: your policies, past jobs, price lists, and know-how in one private store your team can ask and your systems can draw on. It only works if the data under it is clean, which is why it lives in this service.
04

Systems connected end to end

Your booking tool talks to your books, and your books talk to your follow-up. No retyping, no swivel-chair integration.
05

Cut tool sprawl

Most businesses pay for tools nobody opens. We inventory what you have, keep what earns, and cancel the rest. The subscription savings alone often cover a good share of the cleanup.
06

Built to hold AI later

Every field, permission, and connection is set up so agents and automations can be added on top without another rebuild. The foundation is boring on purpose.

Rules to know

AI and data rules in North Carolina.

As of September 2026, North Carolina has not passed a broad AI law or consumer-privacy law that applies to private businesses. Federal rules and your industry's rules still apply.

If you handle health, financial, or children's data, keep it out of public AI tools unless your vendor contract covers it. Private, on-site AI is an option when it can't leave the building.

This is a plain-English summary, not legal advice.

Questions

Straight answers.

Do we need to replace our software first?

Usually not. Most tools Haywood County businesses already run can be connected and cleaned in place. If something truly can't hold the weight, we'll tell you before you spend, not after.

What happens to our old data?

It gets cleaned and moved, with the originals preserved until you've checked the new home. Nothing is deleted on our say-so.

Should we clean our data before adding AI?

Yes, and it's most of the job. An agent reading three conflicting records guesses. An agent reading one clean record, and a Company Brain built on it, answers.

How long does a foundation project take?

Most run two to six weeks, depending on how many systems you run and how messy the records are. You'll get the timeline in writing in the Ops Spec before anything starts.

Do you work with businesses in Waynesville and Haywood County?

Yes. We work remotely, from first call to launch, and we're right here in Clyde when you want us in the room.
