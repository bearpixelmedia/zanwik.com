Hickory at a glance

A furniture-and-making town, carried by mid-size firms.

Catawba County is home to 170,172 people and grew 5.9% between 2020 and 2025. Hickory's furniture-and-manufacturing heritage still anchors the Catawba Valley, and the building keeps coming: the county issued 1,944 building permits in 2025 as growth spreads from Lake Hickory to Maiden and Newton. Its 4,349 employer establishments sit in the middle of it all, making, shipping, selling, and serving across the valley, usually with an office staff smaller than the workload.

Catawba County's business base

Plant schedules, showroom floors, two-person front offices.

Catawba County had 4,349 business locations in 2023, and 1,402 of them had between 10 and 499 employees, together employing 85,704 people. Retail led with 687 locations; other services counted 485, construction 412, accommodation and food service 386, and manufacturing 357, an economy built on making furniture and goods, moving them wholesale, and selling to the valley.

These are the firms that keep the Catawba Valley running: the makers and finish shops, the wholesalers and dealers, the builders following the permits, and the shops and restaurants around Union Square and Viewmont. Their work runs on orders, lead times, and delivery dates set by other people. Miss the follow-up or the paperwork and the order goes to the firm that didn't. That's the work we build for.

What we build here

The slow spots are the same in every Hickory business.

Whatever you run, the drag looks alike: follow-up that slips, quotes that take days, and numbers that live in three places. Here's what we build to fix it. A person approves anything that matters.

01

The diagnostic and the Ops Spec

We map how your business actually runs, find the work that's costing you the most, and put a dollar figure on it. The Zanwik Ops Spec is the written plan that comes out of it: what to build, in what order, and what it should return, for a fixed fee starting at $1,500, yours to keep either way.
02

Systems and data foundation

Clean data and connected systems: one CRM instead of three, records that match across your tools, and integrations between the systems you already pay for. You can't put AI on top of a mess, so we fix the foundation first when it needs fixing.
03

Company Brain

Everything your company knows, in one place your systems can use: your past orders and jobs, your pricing and product rules, your vendor terms, your procedures. Your team asks it questions and gets answers with the source attached, and every agent we build draws on the same knowledge instead of guessing. In a valley where know-how lives with the people who have run the plant and the showroom for years, the Company Brain is what keeps that knowledge working on every order, not just the ones the senior people touch.
04

AI inside your team's work

Drafting, summaries, lookups, and reports handled inside the tools your team already uses, so a lean office gets the output of a bigger one. Nothing new to log into, nothing extra to learn.
05

Agents that handle whole tasks

Workers built around one job each: answering and following up on inquiries, building quotes for a person to approve, processing orders, keeping the calendar. They work inside your tools, follow your rules, and stop for a person on anything that matters.
06

Custom software and integrations

When off-the-shelf software falls short, we build the missing piece: portals, order and booking tools, and the connections between the systems you run on, all on accounts you own.
07

Private and on-premise AI

AI that runs inside your building when records can't go to a cloud tool. Same help, and your data stays home.
08

AI spend and token governance

We watch what the AI itself costs: which tools earn their subscription, where usage is wasted, and when a cheaper model does the same job. No runaway bills, no shelfware.
09

Stay: we run what we built

We stay on after launch: monitoring, fixes, checked work, and a plain report of what the system saved and earned. Systems fail quietly when nobody watches them. Somebody watches yours.

Rules to know

AI and data rules in North Carolina.

We found no broad AI law or consumer-privacy law for North Carolina private businesses as of September 2026. Federal and industry rules still apply.

Keep sensitive customer data out of public AI tools unless your vendor contract covers it.

This is a plain-English summary, not legal advice.
