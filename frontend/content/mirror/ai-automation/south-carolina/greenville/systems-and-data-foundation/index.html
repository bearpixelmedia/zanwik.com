Systems and data in the Upstate

Where Upstate records come apart.

Record problems look different across the Upstate. In Greer and along the interstate, suppliers around BMW and Michelin often quote from one system, track inventory in another and invoice from a third, so the same order gets typed three times and the next quote gets priced from memory.

On the Golden Strip, the trades and home services firms keeping up with growth in Simpsonville and Mauldin tend to run jobs out of a scheduling app, a spreadsheet and the owner's phone. Downtown, professional firms usually have the opposite problem: years of project history spread across shared drives and a few senior people's heads.

The fix starts the same way in each case. We pick the record that matters most, usually the customer or the job, settle which version is right, and connect the systems around it so it stays right. Then anything built on top, from agents to reporting, reads from something true.

What the foundation work covers

You can't put AI on top of a mess.

01

One CRM instead of three

We consolidate the tools that track your customers, jobs, and money into one system your team actually uses, and retire the duplicates you are paying for twice.
02

Records that match

The same customer, the same job, the same dollar figure, identical in every system. We find where records disagree, settle which one is right, and keep them in agreement.
03

Systems connected end to end

Your CRM, accounting, booking, and job data talking to each other, so a change in one place updates the rest and nobody retypes anything.
04

Cut tool sprawl

We audit the software you pay for, then cut, replace, and combine what overlaps. Custom builds only where nothing off-the-shelf fits, so the monthly bill goes down, not up.
05

Data cleaned and moved

Duplicate contacts merged, dead records archived, fields standardized. Migrations are checked record by record before the old system is retired, and nothing gets deleted quietly.
06

Built to hold AI later

Clean fields, sensible permissions, and an audit trail underneath, so when agents, reporting, and knowledge tools land on top, they read from something true. This is the ground your Company Brain stands on: clean, connected records it can actually answer from.

Questions

Straight answers.

Do we need to replace our software first?

Almost never. We start by connecting what you already run. If a tool truly can't connect or is costing you more than it returns, we'll tell you in the assessment, with the numbers.

What happens to our old data?

It comes with you. We migrate and clean it, merge the duplicates, and archive what is dead, and the move is checked record by record before the old system is retired. Nothing gets deleted quietly.

Should we clean our data before adding AI?

Yes. AI reads whatever is underneath it, so duplicate contacts and mismatched job records turn into wrong answers faster. We clean and connect the records first, then build the agents, reporting and knowledge tools on top.

How long does a foundation project take?

It depends on how many systems are involved. We work one system at a time, the first fix is usually live inside the first two weeks, and nothing switches over until the records match.
